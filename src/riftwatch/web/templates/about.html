Method

How RiftWatch works

Most stat sites show you what happened. RiftWatch also measures how it compares with players at your rank, and explains why.

01 · data

Match history from Riot

Search a Riot ID in any region and RiftWatch downloads its ranked and draft games from Riot's API, with each game's minute-by-minute timeline. Games never change once played, so each one is downloaded once and kept.

02 · yardstick

Scored against your rank, in your role

Every minute of a game (farm, gold, experience, vision, the gap to your lane opponent) is compared with players at your rank in the same role, adjusted for champion and matchup. "Better than 70%" means you beat 70% of comparable players on that stat.

03 · coach

An AI coach that can't make things up

The coach only sees computed evidence. Each point it makes must cite that evidence, and a checker removes any point whose numbers don't appear in what it cites.

04 · models

Trained on high-elo play

Decision models learn from thousands of Master+ games what top players do in each situation, and a laning model learns from high-elo replays which trades they take. Each model is scored on games it never saw, and used only where it beats a plain baseline.

05 · meta

Champion statistics

The tier board, builds and matchups come from the {{ '{:,}'.format(ranked) }} ranked games RiftWatch has analysed. Win rates are pulled towards 50% for small samples, so a champion that went 3-0 isn't called a 100% pick. A matchup's Why? is written by the AI coach from the lane numbers and both kits, under the same number check.

06 · notes

What went well, what didn't

Each game gets short notes against three yardsticks: the other nine players, your lane opponent (CS, and gold at 15 minutes), and players at your rank in your role. Rank comparisons count only in the top or bottom fifth, and notes that keep repeating become your Patterns.

07 · comparison

Your game against high-elo choices

The High Elo Comparison replays a game minute by minute, from minute 2 to the end. Each minute shows what you did beside how often Grandmaster and Challenger players chose each option in similar spots, the state of the game, the facts that fit their choice, and what happened next. Those are shares of what top players did, not a promise it would have worked.

08 · patches

Tiers that read the patch notes

Most stored games come from one patch, so champions buffed or nerfed since then get a small nudge (one win-rate point for the games played before the change, less for previews). Changed champions are marked, and the raw tiers are one click away.

09 · limits

What it never does

RiftWatch only uses Riot's public API and Data Dragon. It never reads the game client's memory, never automates play, and doesn't sell or share player data.
